Commercial EPC: a free open data tool from Greener Edge Sustainability

Most of Wales's large commercial buildings aren't at EPC B. By 2031, rented ones are set to need to be.

The UK government plans to require privately rented commercial buildings over 1,000 m² in England and Wales to reach EPC B from 2031. We looked at every current non-domestic EPC in Wales to see how far there is to go.

59%of large buildings (over 1,000 m²) are rated below B: 2,572 buildingsIf rented, each would need upgrading by 2031, where cost-effective, to keep being let.
65%of large buildings last certified for letting are below BThe rented stock, where the rule bites, is further behind than the average.
74%of large industrial and warehouse buildings are below B, the highest of any typeIndustrial units and warehouses face the biggest upgrade task.

In brief

What we found. Most large commercial buildings in Wales are rated below EPC B, and rented ones are further behind. Industrial units, warehouses and offices have the furthest to go, and most of the large buildings below B are heated by gas.

Why it matters. Under the planned rules, a rented building over 1,000 m² rated below B could not be let from 2031 unless upgrading isn't cost-effective. That puts rental income and property values at stake, and upgrades take time to plan and fund.

What you can do here. Read the rules, see which building types have furthest to go, then explore every council.

What the rules say

What this shows: The current minimum standard for letting commercial buildings, and the change planned for 2031.

Why it matters: Every number on this page is measured against this planned standard, so it's the context for everything below.

Today: landlords can't grant or continue a lease on a commercial building rated F or G, unless an exemption applies.

From 2031 (planned): privately rented non-domestic buildings over 1,000 m² must reach EPC B, where cost-effective.

Smaller buildings: the minimum stays at E, with no deadline to go further.

Not final: this comes from the government's interim response of 18 June 2026. It still needs secondary legislation before it takes effect.

Which buildings have furthest to go?

What this shows: Large buildings across Wales by type, split by EPC rating. The darker the bar, the lower the rating; everything right of the line is below B.

Why it matters: It shows which kinds of property face the biggest upgrade task, and where landlords and advisers should look first.

Why it matters: Getting to B often means tackling heating, not just lighting and insulation, which is usually the bigger, slower job.

How we worked

We kept the most recent certificate for each building, matched on property reference (UPRN) where available, otherwise on address, and counted certificates lodged in the 10 years before download as current. "Large" means a floor area over 1,000 m². "Last certified for letting" means the latest certificate was issued because the property was being let. Building types are our grouping of the register's property categories.

Limits

The register only holds buildings that have needed an EPC, so it isn't every building in Wales. The data can't tell us which buildings are rented today, so the 2031 figures show buildings that would be in scope if rented. Certificates may be out of date where work has been done since. The 2031 standard is planned, not yet law. Figures for councils with few buildings of a type are shown as not enough data.
