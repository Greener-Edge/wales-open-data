Analysis by Greener Edge Sustainability, using official EPC register data

Most of Wales's big commercial buildings aren't at EPC B. By 2031, rented ones will need to be.

The UK government plans to require privately rented commercial buildings over 1,000 m² in England and Wales to reach EPC B from 2031. We looked at every current non-domestic EPC in Wales to see how far there is to go.

59%of large buildings (over 1,000 m²) are rated below B. That's 2,572 buildings.
65%of large buildings last certified for letting are below B
74%of large industrial and warehouse buildings are below B, the highest of any type
61%of large buildings below B are heated mainly by gas

What the rules say

Today: landlords can't grant or continue a lease on a commercial building rated F or G, unless an exemption applies.

From 2031 (planned): privately rented non-domestic buildings over 1,000 m² must reach EPC B, where cost-effective.

Smaller buildings: the minimum stays at E, with no deadline to go further.

Not final: this comes from the government's interim response of June 2026. It still needs secondary legislation, and a full response is expected.

Source: Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government, Energy Performance of Buildings Data for England and Wales, non-domestic certificates, downloaded 2 October 2026. No address data is shown or published here.

Our working: we kept the most recent certificate for each building, matched on property reference (UPRN) where available, otherwise on address. We counted certificates lodged in the last 10 years as current. "Large" means a floor area over 1,000 m². "Last certified for letting" means the latest certificate was issued because the property was being let. Building types are our grouping of the register's property categories.

Limits: the register only holds buildings that have needed an EPC, so it isn't every building in Wales. The data can't tell us which buildings are rented today, so the 2031 figures show buildings that would be in scope if rented. Certificates may be out of date where work has been done since. Figures for councils with few buildings of a type are shown as not enough data.
