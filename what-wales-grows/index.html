Analysis by Greener Edge Sustainability, using Welsh Government farming statistics

85% of Welsh farmland is grass. Fruit and vegetables use less than 0.1%.

Wales farms 1.77 million hectares. Most of it feeds sheep and cattle, because that's what the land, soil and rain suit best. Here's what Wales grows, how that's changed since 1867, and how it compares with what the UK imports.

What Wales grows, and what the UK buys in

Welsh farming is built on grass, livestock, milk and eggs. The UK's most climate-exposed imports are the foods that use the least Welsh land: two-thirds of imported fruit and nuts, and 61% of vegetables, come from water-stressed countries. Wales can't grow citrus or cocoa, but the gap raises a fair question about what more could be grown closer to home.

Our working: grass includes grassland under and over five years old and sole-rights rough grazing. Fruit and vegetables include vegetables and salad grown in the open, commercial orchards, other orchards and small fruit, and glasshouses. Limits: survey estimates are based on the farms that respond, scaled up to represent all farms. The 2025 response rate was 50%.
