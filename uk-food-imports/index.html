Two-thirds of the UK's imported fruit and nuts come from water-stressed countries.

The UK imports around $72 billion of food a year. We mapped where it comes from, and checked each origin against water stress, drought, flooding and deforestation, today and into the future.

The UK's biggest food imports, by risk

Each bubble is one of the UK's 70 biggest food imports, sized by what the UK spends on it. The ring shows which of four risks it faces, and the stronger the colour, the more risks. Select a food group to zoom in, select a food for its details, or sort everything by exposure.

A risk is flagged when over half of a food comes from water-stressed countries, over half from drought-risk countries, over half from its single biggest supplier, or when its crop carries more than 500 ha of deforestation risk. Press Escape or select the background to zoom back out. On a phone, swipe the chart sideways to see it all.

Full methods, sources and licences for the whole series are on the Methods page.

Our working: we grouped FAOSTAT's 425 products into food types and removed non-food items such as tobacco, cotton, wool, live animals and pet food. "Water-stressed" means Aqueduct's high (40-80%) or extremely high (over 80%) bands. "Drought risk" means its medium-high or high drought bands. Deforestation for processed foods, such as chocolate or soy oil, is shown for the whole crop family (all cocoa, all soy), because the deforestation data is measured at crop level. Supplier concentration is the share of UK imports of a food from its single biggest supplier. Values are in US dollars, as FAOSTAT reports them.

Limits: trade figures show the country recorded as the partner, which can be where food was processed or shipped from rather than grown. Belgium and the Netherlands, for example, handle a lot of food grown elsewhere. Country water stress scores are averages, so a supplier may farm in a less stressed region. Deforestation figures are national averages that measure risk exposure, not proof that a particular product caused clearing.
