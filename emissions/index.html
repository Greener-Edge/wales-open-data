What did the heavy lifting?

Change in Welsh emissions between 2005 and 2024, grouped by source. Falling electricity emissions did twice as much as large industrial sites. Shares are of the total 20.2 million tonne fall.

Electricity covers power used by homes, businesses, industry, farms and the public sector. The fall reflects both a cleaner grid and lower use; the data doesn't separate the two. Groupings are our own, built from the DESNZ sub-sectors.

One place, one year

Neath Port Talbot is home to 4.5% of people in Wales but produced 17.6% of its emissions in 2024, mostly from large industrial sites. When the last blast furnaces at Port Talbot closed, Welsh emissions dropped by 2.1 million tonnes in a single year, almost all of it here.

The closures happened part way through 2024, so we'd expect more of the effect to show in the 2025 figures, due from DESNZ in June 2027. Territorial statistics only count what happens inside Wales, so emissions from steel made elsewhere and used here don't appear.

What's included: territorial emissions of CO₂, methane and nitrous oxide. Aviation, shipping, military transport and fluorinated gases are excluded. Electricity and fuel supply emissions are assigned to where the energy is used. Land use and forestry can be negative, where land absorbs more carbon than it releases.

Our working: per person figures divide emissions by mid-year population. Driver groupings and the Neath Port Talbot shares are Greener Edge calculations from the published figures. DESNZ advises that local figures be interpreted with care, as councils have limited influence over some sources.
