Wales emissions: a free open data tool from Greener Edge Sustainability

Wales has nearly halved its greenhouse gas emissions since 2005.

Emissions fell from 43.8 to 23.5 million tonnes CO₂e between 2005 and 2024. Most of that came from a cleaner electricity grid and less waste sent to landfill, not from closing industry.

In brief

What we found. Welsh emissions have fallen steadily since 2005. The biggest drivers were electricity (a cleaner grid and lower use) and landfill, with large industrial sites third. The 2023 to 2024 drop was different: almost all of it came from the closure of Port Talbot's last blast furnaces.

Why it matters. Wales has a legal target of net zero by 2050, and about half of 2005's emissions remain. Closures cut Welsh figures, but the products still get made somewhere, so they don't cut global emissions in the same way. The harder, lasting cuts are still ahead in transport, homes, farming and industry.

What did the heavy lifting?

What this shows: How much each source of emissions changed between 2005 and 2024, grouped by type. Shares are of the total 20.2 million tonne fall.

Why it matters: It separates lasting progress (cleaner power, less landfill) from one-off drops like closures, which matters when judging whether Wales is on track.

Electricity covers power used by homes, businesses, industry, farms and the public sector. The fall reflects both a cleaner grid and lower use; the data doesn't separate the two. Groupings are our own, built from the DESNZ sub-sectors.

One place, one year

What this shows: Total Welsh emissions, Wales without Neath Port Talbot, and Neath Port Talbot itself, from 2005 to 2024.

Why it matters: Neath Port Talbot is home to 4.5% of people in Wales but produced 17.6% of its emissions in 2024, mostly from large industrial sites. When the last blast furnaces closed, Welsh emissions dropped by 2.1 million tonnes in a single year, almost all of it here.

The closures happened part way through 2024, so we'd expect more of the effect to show in the 2025 figures, due from DESNZ in June 2027. Territorial statistics only count what happens inside Wales, so emissions from steel made elsewhere and used here don't appear.

How we worked

Per person figures divide emissions by mid-year population. Driver groupings and the Neath Port Talbot shares are Greener Edge calculations from the published DESNZ sub-sectors. We checked our Wales totals against the DESNZ statistical release.

Limits

These are territorial emissions of CO₂, methane and nitrous oxide. Aviation, shipping, military transport and fluorinated gases are excluded. Electricity and fuel supply emissions are assigned to where the energy is used, and the electricity fall combines a cleaner grid with lower use. Land use and forestry can be negative, where land absorbs more carbon than it releases.

The Port Talbot closures happened part way through 2024, so we'd expect more of the effect in the 2025 figures. DESNZ advises that local figures be interpreted with care, as councils have limited influence over some sources.
